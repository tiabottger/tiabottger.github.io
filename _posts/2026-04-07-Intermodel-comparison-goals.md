## Intermodel comparison goals summary
On April 2, 2026 we had a meeting to get the ball rolling on an intermodel comparison between LiveOcean, UBC's SalishSeaCast, and the Salish Sea Model. I want to use this blog post to summarise some important take aways and possible next steps.

The fundamental question we are trying to get at is whether changing nutrient input through wastewater treatment plants results in a difference in the occurance of hypoxia. This question relies on leaving the underlying hydrodynamics the same and only changing nutrients. As a result, it's important to understand differences in how the models simulate hydrodynamics as a first step before changing an input which there are no real life measurements to compare model results to. Irby et al. 2016 found that even models with the barest bones of biogeochemistry were able to simulate dissolved oxygen levels as well as those with complex biogeochem modules. And from Aurora's work we know hydrodynamic characteristics like residence time and exchange flow seem to be the most important predictors of low DO.

Susan Allen made the important point that we can start with the question of why we see a different result starting from looking at oxygen and nutrients which motivate our work, which can then bring us to questioning differences in the models' hydrodynamics. The processes are connected, and we could spend forever just in the hydrodynamics world and tweaking and improving model configurations. Instead, we should start with a comparison as is and then can go back for a deeper dive into model improvement seperately. Model skill metrics in doing a mod-mod-mod-obs comparison can then help answer why we see differences.

We don't want to change atmospheric or river forcing configurations because having some diversity in this creates a more robust comparison. However we do want to be as alligned as possible on WWTP dischargers and river nutrient inputs. Aurora has added TRAPS (tiny rivers and point sources) to LiveOcean following the sources Ecology uses for Salish Sea Model. However this does not include Canadian wastewater treatment plants. Because we are focused on the Puget Sound this might be okay, Susan suggested doing a dye tracer experiment in their model to see how for Canadian sources travel/if they enter Puget Sound.

We want to run multiple years and the same years for best comparison. Salish Sea Model is fundamentally different in how it is run, as it runs the same year over and over again to spin up conditions and has been mostly focused on 2014. Salish Sea Cast and LiveOcean have runs available 2015 forward. Salish Sea Model uses boundary conditions files that Eclology and Turang have developed, and it seemed including more years would require reaching out for these boundary conditions files rather than Mike Brett and team creating our own so that Ecology has confidence in them. It's important that we are comparing to Salish Sea Model as Ecology runs it, since this is what the Wastewater treatment discharger regulations are set based on.

Finally, the difficulty of comparing with different grid configurations became clear in this meeting.
