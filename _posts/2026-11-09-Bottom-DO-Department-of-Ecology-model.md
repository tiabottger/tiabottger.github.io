## Difference in Ecology timing

The January hypoxia in the SSM PNNL model but not the Department of Ecology model version caused us to wonder whether other variables appeared shifted in seasonality between the models. Aurora said Ben that in a previous version of SSM Ben had discovered that in the Kitsap peninsula (coming into Hood Canal) the river inputs started on the water year rather than the year, causing a shift in timing. Because most of hypoxic area seems to be occurring in Hood Canal, this could effect a significant difference. To investigate this, I plotted surface salinity fields for both versions of Salish Sea Model, along with the surface salinity averaged over the Hood Canal domain.

<p style="text-align:center;"><video src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ecbff2e4-a6e5-4a42-8d12-e5e19d22f1eb" controls="controls" style="max-width: 800px;"></video>
 </p>

The timing in surface salinity fields seems to be the same between the two models. It is interesting that they are not identical, however. In particular, there is a period from August to November in which the PNNL model is markedly fresher in Hood Canal than Ecology's version. Hood Canal is not the only part of the Sound where there is a difference in surface salinities, PNNL's version seems to have more freshwater around the Skagit and Snohomish rivers as well. In this period, Ecology's model has more hypoxic area (mostly concentrated in Hood Canal) than PNNL's model. Meanwhile, in January Ecology's model is slightly fresher and we see hypoxic area in PNNL's model. More freshwater could correspond to more flushing/mixing, lessening hypoxic area during these periods. 

This does not seem to explain the difference in hypoxic area timing between the two models. The next step is to reach out to Lily Engel and ask if folks at PNNL have any hypotheses as to why there is hypoxia in January when this doesn't show up in other models.
