TEF integrates over isohalines (contours of constant salinity) instead of physical distance. TEF is therefore transport as a function of salinity, resulting in how much salty water flows in and how much fresher estuarine water flows out.

What are the important terms in TEF?
- $Q_in$ total inflow transport
- $Q_out$ total outflow transport
- $Q_net$ net volume transport
- $S_in$ inflow salinity
- $S_out$ outflow salinity
- $Q_in /delta S$ exchange-driven salt transport. $= Q_in(S_in - S_out)$. Physically, this is how much salt is carried into the estuary by the exchange flow. If the inflow and outflow have the same salinity ($/delta S = 0)$ then there is no transport of salt by the exchange flow.

**Top panel**: volumetric flow rates of **estuarine exchange flow**. The exchange transport has a magnitude between 5 to 10 x 10^-3 m^3/s. The lines are the sum of all inflows and outflow, essentially simplifying the exchange flow into a two-layer flow. The dots show individual inflow and outflow of each layer of the exchange flow. Places where there is only one dot are where the exchange flow is exactly two layers, these occur where the exchange flow is strongest during neap tide. The red line is inflow, while the blue line is outflow. Inflow and outflow balance eachother by conservation of mass.

**Middle panel**: **salinity**. During spring tide when the water column is more well mixed, the salinity of the inflow and outflow are equivalent and transport is tidally driven. During neap tide stratification sets up and the salinity of inflow (salty water at depth) is higher that the fresher surface outflow, around 29 g/kg compared to 18 g/kg. This confirms that the periods of true two layer flow captured in the top panel are driven by the salinity gradient and are truly magnitudes of estuarine exchange flow.
