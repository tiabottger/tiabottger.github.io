## TEF on idealized estuary
The Total Exchange Flow (TEF) framework integrates over isohalines (contours of constant salinity) instead of physical distance. TEF is therefore transport as a function of salinity, resulting in how much salty water flows in and how much fresher estuarine water flows out.

Important terms in TEF:
- $Q_{in}$ total inflow transport
- $Q_{out}$ total outflow transport
- $Q_{net}$ net volume transport
- $S_{in}$ average inflow salinity, weighted by how much transport occurs at each salinity
- $S_{out}$ average outflow salinity, weighted as above
- $Q_{in} \Delta S = Q_{in}(S_{in} - S_{out})$ exchange-driven salt transport. Physically, this is how much salt is carried into the estuary by the exchange flow. If the inflow and outflow have the same salinity ($\Delta S = 0)$ then there is no transport of salt by the exchange flow.

In the calculation of TEF, transport data are binned into salinity classes. A transport per salinity bin is calculated. Inflow transport is the sum of all the positive salinity bin transports, while outflow transport is the sum of all the negative salinity bin transports. Because TEF is defined in salinity space, you can have both inflow and outflow occurring at the same physical location but seperated by salinity class.

**Top panel**: volumetric flow rates of **estuarine exchange flow**. The exchange transport has a magnitude between 5 to 10 x 10^-3 m^3/s for the inflow and 6 to 11 x 10^-3 m^3/s for the outflow. The lines are the sum of all inflows and outflow, essentially simplifying the exchange flow into a two-layer flow. The dots show individual inflow and outflow of each layer of the exchange flow. Places where there is only one dot are where the exchange flow is exactly two layers, these occur where the exchange flow is strongest during neap tide. The red line is inflow, while the blue line is outflow.

**Middle panel**: **salinity**. During spring tide when the water column is more well mixed, the salinity of the inflow and outflow are equivalent and transport is tidally driven. During neap tide stratification sets up and the salinity of inflow (salty water at depth) is higher that the fresher surface outflow, around 29 g/kg compared to 18 g/kg. This confirms that the periods of true two layer flow captured in the top panel are driven by the salinity gradient and are truly magnitudes of estuarine exchange flow.

**Bottom panel**: **exchange-driven salt transport and tidal prism**. $Q_{prism}$ is the maximum possible exchange flow if all the flood tide made $Q_{in}$ and the ebb tide made $Q_{out}$. So this tern represents the volume transport due to only the flooding and ebbing of tides. It is plotted with $Q_{in} \Delta S$, which is the transort of salt by the exchange flow. When the tidal prism is at its maximum, there is zero transport of salt by the exchange flow and we can assume that the tides are causing sloshing back and forth of well mixed water such that the salinity of the inflow is the same as the salinity of the outflow. When the tidal prism is at a minimum and we expect the least transport due to tidal energy, two-layer estuarine exchange sets up and there is a net salt transport of approximately 90 g/kg m^3/s.
