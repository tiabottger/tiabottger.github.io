## Timeseries and distribution plots
## Timeseries 
I've plotted timeseires pulling the closest model value to match observations at 1 m and 30.5 m depth, looking at a station in each region of Puget Sound. LiveOcean tends to predict higher values for DO than SalishSeaCast. You can see the seasonal variability, with oxygen concentrations decreasing as spring blooms die out at the end of summer. There is greater variability closer to the surface, where oxygen is higher due to exchange with the atmosphere and photosynthesis. The models have the closest agreement in Main basin- Admiralty inlet, and some of the biggest differences in Whidbey basin- Saratoga passage.

## Distribution plots- 2014 compared to other years
We have been asking the question of what our expectations are to use the year 2014 as a comparison year to more broadly describe models. In particular, the potential of still having spin-up effects in 2014 made us concerned that the models are not representing observational data as well in this year. To further investigate this question I made distribution plots comparing 2014 to other years. The first plot shows the distribution of model versus observational data. The line represents the average value, and we can see both models underpredict DO on average. LiveOcean is biased higher than SalishSeaCast. The second plot shows error as observations - model values. 2014 appears representative in terms of the error distribution. The average values of SalishSeaCast and LiveOcean error are skewed positive, with observations being larger than model values.
