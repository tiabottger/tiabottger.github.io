## Comparing profiles in Whidbey basin

Whidbey basin seems to have the largest, most striking differences in bottom DO between LiveOcean and SalishSeaCast. One thought is that the model's differences in representing bathymetry where large rivers enter Whidbey basin is causing this incoming freshwater to mix or circulate differently between the models, with different stratification structures. Bathymetry differences or no, Whidbey basin is highly freshwater influenced, and we have hypothesized that perhaps differences in model's representation of the stratification is leading to different DO dynamics here as compared to the more well-mixed Main basin. The next step in exploring the differences arising in Whidbey basin is therefore to compare the vertical structure of the water column at a couple of sites in Whidbey basin (we don't have the full vertical extraction for more than a couple of sites, although we could do more extractions). 

Of the sites in Whidbey basin that we have extractions for, SKG003 seems to have the biggest differences between the two models. This is the site which is closest to the Skagit river and flats, which we know are not as resolved in SalishSeaCast. From previous comparison to observations, we know that SalishSeaCast had a greater RMSE in representing DO here than LiveOcean.

Comparing density profiles, **SalishSeaCast is more strongly stratified than LiveOcean**. We think LiveOcean may be consistently overpredicting near surface salinity, and that LiveOcean may be less stratified than actual conditions. However, LiveOcean seems to be predicting DO more correctly than SalishSeaCast. Is it possible that higher stratification in SalishSeaCast is suppressing vertical mixing causing low DO bottom water to stick around? It was surprising to see that low DO in SalishSeaCast Whidbey basin doesn't just arise at the end of summer, but seems to be already present from the previous year even in the spring. However this can't be the only mechanism at play, especially if we think LiveOcean stratification is incorrect but it is more correctly predicting DO.

Bottom DO is consistently lower at SAR003 and PSS019 in SalishSeaCast, matching the maps of bottom DO in Whidbey basin. The SKG003 site is interesting because it doesn't match this trend. It is also a much shallower site so dynamics here are different in general. 

Looking at the profiles also points to some potential biogeochemical differences between the models. In the SKG003 site this is especially evident. In LiveOcean, the highest oxygen concentrations occur near the surface, whereas SalishSeaCast exhibits a maxima around 5 meters. While it is less obvious at the other sites, SalishSeaCast tends to have a DO maximum lower in the water column than LiveOcean. Looking at the chlorophyll profiles, it seems like phytoplankton in SalishSeaCast also tend to be concentrated lower in the water column, with a sub-surface "bump". This made us wonder whether factors suppressing phytoplankton growth right at the surface, such as photo-inhibition, may be stronger in SalishSeaCast than LiveOcean. In general LiveOcean seems to have much higher chlorophyll concentrations, and the observational data doesn't particularly clarify which model's concentrations are more believable. This is a reminder of how large phytoplankton variability can be especially close to the surface, with samples taken just a little higher/lower in the water column potentially returning quite different chlorophyll results.
