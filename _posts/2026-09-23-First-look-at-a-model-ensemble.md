## First look at a model ensemble
A big update to share this week! Salish Sea Model output data is available at the Salish Sea Modeling Center data portal: https://ssmc-uw.org/data-portal/. Thank you to Lily Engel from PNNL to pointing us here. Data is available for not only 2014, but 2013-2024. I've extracted 2014 data as a starting place, the question remains of whether Department of Ecology has used its own configuration of Salish Sea Model for their 2014 analysis or whether they have used the same functional model as the data provided here including other years as well. It is amazing to be able to move into doing a multi-year analysis, even if Ecology uses a different version of Salish Sea Model we could end up treating this as a distinct model-- in other words a 3+1 model comparison of three comparable models and one "management" model for 2014 only.

Having the full resolution of Salish Sea Model data output allowed me to match model data to our larger observational dataset. And, for the first time be able to calculate a model mean between all three models. This gets to a fundamental question, is a model ensemble containing more information than one model alone a better predictor of dissolved oxygen? Each model has made its own choices and parameterizations, and each model has error. When looking at a spatial map of which model has the least RMSE at any given station location, there is no clear "winner" and different models have strengths at different sites (Figure 1). As a result, taken together, a model ensemble using the mean of models performs best overall, with the exception of a few stations (Figure 2).

The RMSE taking into account all stations for the model ensemble is 1.252 mg/L, followed by LiveOcean's 1.289 mg/L, Salish Sea Model's 1.447 mg/L and SalishSeaCast's 1.575 mg/L. I think there are a couple of stations waiting SalishSeaCast's RMSE lower-- the northmost station in Whidbey basin near the Skagit flats has large error due to the masking of the Skagit flats effecting how the Skagit river enters Whidbey basin. Where this station is located is right at the edge of the grid that SalishSeaCast uses, with the Skagit flats masked out. Also, the station near Port Townsend by Indian Island has large error. This is because the small channel between Indian Island and the mainland is not included in the grid, so the model is representing this area as a terminal inlet causing DO predictions to be much lower, when it really has connectivity. I think the way the rivers are entering in South Sound in SalishSeaCast may also be causing a difference, as the rivers aren't included as part of the grid.

Because the same areas tend to be the hardest for all models to represent, the model mean still has high RMSE in a couple of locations. Lynch cove in Hood canal remains difficult to capture, along with several shallow stations in Lynch cove. I think the shallow stations are more difficult to represent because sediment dynamics play a larger role here where the water column is well mixed. Sediment dynamics are quite complex, and over simplified in the models. Tall has mentioned that the bathymetry in Lynch cove is difficult to capture. It makes sense that because the channel is so narrow here, getting the bathymetry right with a courser grid resolution is difficult-- resulting in a difference in the dynamics that propagates to the end of the cove where all models have the hardest time matching observations. I'd like to investigate the profiles of DO here, as this areas shows higher DO variability making it harder to capture, but I think the variability is largely vertical rather than temporal as Lynch cove remains hypoxic for much of the year. There is also a strong seiche that may result in temporal variability depending on where the internal wave is, not be captured in the models.

Taking a difference between each model RMSE and the mean RMSE reveals that overall the mean tends to have less error than each individual model (more blue, Figure 3). This is certainly the case for SalishSeaCast and Salish Sea Model, while LiveOcean seems to be more along the margin of improvement. It's interesting how SalishSeaCast seems to have the best predictions in Admiralty inlet and the entrance to Hood canal, as this model has been optimized for Canadian waters, and those are perhaps the stations with the most connectivity to the Strait of Georgia.
